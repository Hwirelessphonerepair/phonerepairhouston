What a Good Repair Shop Does First: Diagnose

The same symptom can have several causes. A phone that won't charge might have a worn cable, lint packed into the port, a weak battery or an internal fault. A black screen after a drop could be a broken display, or it could be a phone that has stopped powering on. The Most Common Phone Repairs and What They Involve

Cracked or unresponsive screens

A phone screen has layers: the outer glass, the touch layer (digitizer) and the display panel underneath. A crack in the glass alone is different from damage that causes black spots, lines, flickering or "ghost" touches. On many newer phones, especially iPhones with OLED displays, these layers are bonded, so the fix is usually a full display assembly replacement.

Battery that drains fast or shuts down

Lithium-ion batteries wear out with use. Common signs include rapid drain, shutdowns at 20% or 30%, or a phone that only stays on while plugged in. A new battery often makes an older phone usable again. A swollen battery that pushes the screen up is a safety issue, so stop charging the phone and have it checked.

Charging port problems

If the cable feels loose or only charges at an angle, the port may just be full of pocket lint. Cleaning it can solve the problem without replacing anything. Worn or corroded contacts need a port repair or replacement.

Back glass, cameras and water damage

Cracked back glass exposes the inside of the phone to dust and moisture. Camera faults can show up as blurry photos, a black camera screen or a flash that doesn't work. Water damage is the hardest to judge from the outside, because liquid can affect the screen, battery, charging system and main board in different ways. It always needs an internal inspection.

Repair or Replace?

Repair usually makes sense when the phone is otherwise in good shape and the fault is limited to one part, such as the screen, battery or charging port. Replacement is worth considering when several major parts have failed at once, when the phone no longer gets software updates, or when it has severe water damage. A good shop should tell you honestly if a repair isn't practical.

Questions to Ask Before You Approve a Repair

  1. What exactly is wrong? Ask what the technician found and how they tested it.
  2. Which part will you use? A good shop explains the replacement part options for your model.
  3. How long will it take? Many common repairs can be done the same day if the part is in stock, but no honest shop guarantees that for every repair.
  4. What does the warranty cover? At H Wireless, a limited warranty covers defective parts and workmanship on eligible repairs. New accidental damage, liquid damage, third-party tampering and unrelated faults are not covered.
  5. Is my data safe? Ask how the shop handles your device and whether it needs your passcode to test the repair.
Before you drop off your phone: back it up if it still turns on, remove the case, and bring your charging cable if the problem is charging-related. Make a note of when the problem started and anything that happened beforehand, such as a drop, a spill or a new charger.
